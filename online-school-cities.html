The model

What stays the same wherever you are

Live, not recorded

Small cohorts taught live by named facilitators. Recorded content turns a classroom into a video library and leaves the child alone with it — which is exactly how online schooling fails.

1:8 mentors, and a monthly meet-up

Every learner has a mentor who knows their work and their week, with daily reporting to parents. The local cohort also meets in person once a month, so friendships are not only on screen.

AI, code and entrepreneurship

Technology is part of the curriculum, taught with disclosure, verification and judgement — not as an optional club and not something to be banned.

Recognised board pathways

Guided preparation toward NIOS, Cambridge IGCSE (private candidate) or GED, examined by the official board through its own centres.

Parent questions

City and time-zone questions

Because Hash Future School teaches live and online, your city does not limit your options - your time zone does. We group cohorts by region, so families in Bengaluru, Mumbai, Dubai, Doha or Riyadh all study the same programme with the same 1:8 mentor ratio and the same board pathways, in slots that fit their local day.

Indian cities study on IST inside normal school hours. Gulf cohorts are grouped at Gulf Standard Time or Arabia Standard Time, so children in Dubai, Doha, Riyadh and Kuwait City study in the late afternoon or early evening rather than at midnight. Timings are published per cohort before you enrol.

Yes, and nothing about the academic programme changes. Your child keeps the same cohort, the same facilitators and the same board pathway; only the session time may shift. That continuity is the main reason relocating and expat families choose a live programme over a school place.

It depends on where your child is likely to study next and how they handle examinations. NIOS is usually the most practical route for families returning to India, IGCSE fits international university plans, and GED suits older students who want a faster test-based credential. We map it backwards from the destination.
