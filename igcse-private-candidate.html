The basics

What "private candidate" actually means

A private candidate is a learner who sits Cambridge examinations without being enrolled at a Cambridge-registered school. The exam centre enters your child for the papers, supervises the exams, and the certificate is issued by Cambridge International — exactly the same qualification a school candidate receives.

What the centre does not do is teach your child. That is the gap most families hit: they find an exam centre, then realise nobody is going to prepare the student. That preparation is what we do.

Where we stand, plainly: Hash Future School is not a Cambridge-registered school and does not claim to be. Your child registers as a private candidate through a Cambridge-approved centre. We handle the teaching, the study plan and the preparation, and we help you coordinate the centre and deadlines.

Step by step

How Indian families take the IGCSE private route

1. Decide subjects from the destination backwards

Most universities expect at least five IGCSEs including English and Mathematics, and specific degrees require specific sciences or a second language. We start with your child's target country, degree or career, then build the subject list — for example Mathematics (0580) and First Language English (0500) as anchors, with sciences and humanities around them. Syllabus codes and availability are always confirmed against the current Cambridge directory for your chosen exam series.

2. Find a centre that accepts private candidates

British Council centres and a number of registered international schools in India accept private candidates, but not every centre accepts every subject. For Kerala families we identify the nearest centre that covers your child's exact subject list.

3. Handle registration and entry deadlines early

Cambridge entries close months before the exam series. Miss the window and your child waits for the next one, which can cost six months. We build the entire academic calendar backwards from the centre's entry deadline.

4. Plan for practicals and session details

Science subjects involve practical assessment, and Cambridge provides alternative arrangements for some private candidates. We confirm exactly which option your centre offers before your child starts the syllabus.

5. Teach the syllabus, then drill the papers

Mentor-led classes cover the syllabus, and then shift into past papers, mark schemes and examiner expectations. This is where IGCSE results are actually decided — the exam asks for structured answers that most Indian students have never been taught to write.

6. Build the portfolio alongside the certificate

A strong international application is not a set of grades. It is AI and coding capability, real projects, published work, public speaking and evidence of initiative. Our students build all of that while preparing for the board.

  • Mentor-led teaching across the IGCSE syllabus
