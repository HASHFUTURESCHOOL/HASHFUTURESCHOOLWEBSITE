· A note from the school

We said we would go by what learners do. Here is the evidence.

This quarter Hash Future School opened its academic year — thirteen cohorts, learners from six to eighteen, twenty-one facilitators, and a first proper full quarter of running an online school at scale with our own platform underneath it.

An impact report can easily become a brochure. We have tried to make this one an audit instead. It reports the things that went well and the things that did not, side by side, because the second list is what tells us where to spend next quarter.

So: 1,487 classes were scheduled and 1,129 of them were delivered. Learners submitted 2,851 pieces of work. Almost 300,000 points were awarded — and the largest single category of those points was not marks, it was camera on, followed by punctuality and simply submitting the assignment. 185 badges were earned, and most of them for habits rather than grades. Learners ran their own weekly webinars, ran their own election with thirty-eight positions, and entered forty-five external competitions between them.

They also told us, 3,583 times, how a class actually felt — and rated it 4.62 out of 5. But when we asked how challenged they felt, the honest answer was "not very". That finding is in this report, unedited, and it is the first thing we are fixing.

Thank you to the families who trusted us with a school year.

11 What's next

Five things this quarter told us to fix.

An impact report that only counts wins is a marketing document. These are the gaps this data exposed, each with the number that exposed it and the target we are setting for Q2.

Gap 01 · Marks returned late
9.8 days

Average time between a learner submitting work and receiving a grade. August grading completion fell to 63%, and 549 pieces of work were never graded at all. Q2 target: 90% of work graded within 72 hours, and no cohort carrying a backlog across a month.

Gap 02 · Attendance
71.7 %

Participation is steady but not good enough: 3,356 absences in a quarter, and a 27-point gap between the strongest cohort (87.6%) and the weakest (60.8%). Q2 target: 80% participation, with a named owner for every learner below 60%.

Gap 03 · Not enough challenge
1.9 / 5

Learners rate how challenged they felt at 1.9 while rating enjoyment at 4.2 — they are happy and comfortable, and comfort is not stretch. Q2 target: publish a stretch tier on every activity and move the challenge score above 3.

Gap 04 · Retention
22 left

Twenty-two learners left and thirteen joined, so the school grew slowly while working hard. Attrition is the highest-leverage number in this report. Q2 target: an exit conversation with every family, and each reason coded into the platform.

Gap 05 · Support responsiveness
13.5 days

Average time to close a support ticket, with 52 tickets still open at quarter end. In a school where a parent's question is usually about their child's day, two weeks is too long. Q2 target: first response inside one working day, closure inside three.

And the groundwork for what came next

Two capabilities were built and validated during this quarter and switched on immediately after it closed: an adaptive AI quiz engine that adjusts question difficulty in real time, and a Deep Dive practice environment. In the first two weeks of September they generated 729 adaptive sessions and 21,557 answered questions. They are not counted anywhere in this report — they are next quarter's story.

How the data was produced

Reproducible, source-of-truth, no estimates.

No figure in this report is modelled, projected or estimated. Each is a direct aggregate of records in the school's own database, retrieved read-only through the school's MCP interface and aggregated by period. Where a metric could be read two ways, the more conservative reading is used.
